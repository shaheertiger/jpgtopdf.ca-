Scan to PDF — Turn Phone Photos of Paper Into a Clean PDF

Take a photo of each page, pick a filter, download one PDF.

Scan to PDF

Quick answer

Tap Take a photo (or choose photos you already have), shoot each page, and press Download PDF. The default Greyscale filter stretches the contrast so paper looks white; Black & white removes shadows and gives the smallest file. Pages default to Letter or A4 for your region. There is no automatic edge detection, so frame each page tightly.

How to scan a document to PDF with your phone

  1. Set the page down well. A dark, flat surface and daylight, with no shadow from your phone across the paper.
  2. Tap Take a photo. Your phone's rear camera opens. Hold the phone straight above the page and fill the frame with the paper; this matters most, because the tool does not crop or straighten for you.
  3. Add the other pages. Tap Take a photo again for each page, or choose several photos from your library.
  4. Pick a filter. Greyscale suits most documents. Black & white is best for plain text, forms and receipts. Keep Colour for IDs, stamps and anything with colour that matters.
  5. Check the order and download. Drag thumbnails to reorder, tap ↻ to fix a sideways page, then press Download PDF.

Which filter should I use?

FilterWhat it doesFile sizeBest for
ColourKeeps the photo as it isLargestIDs, passports, coloured forms, photos
GreyscaleRemoves colour and stretches contrast so the darkest 2% becomes black and the lightest 2% whiteAbout 740 KB a page (Balanced), 320 KB (Small)Letters, contracts, most documents
Black & whiteAdaptive threshold: each pixel is compared with its neighbourhood, which removes shadows and uneven light. Saved as a 1-bit imageAbout 95 KB a pageText pages, forms, receipts, notes

Sizes are from our test with full Letter pages of printed text; busy pages and photos come out larger. Black & white can wash out light pencil, photos or signatures in pale ink; switch to Greyscale if part of the page disappears.

Is this as good as a scanner app?

For clean, well-lit pages, the result is close. Built-in scanners such as iPhone Notes and Google Drive also find the page edges and straighten the perspective automatically, which this tool does not do. If you can, take the photo straight on, and crop in your camera app first if the table shows around the page. Our guide on scanning with the phone's built-in scanner covers those apps.

What this tool adds: it works in any browser without installing anything, it handles photos you've already taken, and nothing is sent to a cloud account.

How do I make a scanned PDF searchable?

A scan is a picture of text, so you can't search or copy it yet. Run the PDF through OCR PDF, which adds an invisible text layer over each page.

Use cases

IRCC and visa uploads (about 4 MB)

The default Balanced quality keeps pages readable while keeping the file small. If a multi-page scan is still over the limit, switch to Small or use Compress PDF.

Signed forms sent back by email

Print, sign, photograph each page and send one PDF. Gmail attachments are limited to 25 MB, which a Balanced scan of a long form stays far below.

Receipts for expenses

Black & white makes faded thermal receipts more readable and keeps a month of receipts small.

School and university submissions

Handwritten homework photographed page by page becomes one PDF that portals accept.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scan to PDF free?

Yes, with no account, no watermark and no page limit beyond your device's memory.

Are my scans uploaded anywhere?

No. Photos are processed and the PDF is built in your browser; nothing is sent to a server.

Do I need to install an app?

No. It runs in your phone's browser and opens the camera directly.

Does it straighten or crop the page automatically?

No. There is no edge detection or perspective correction, so fill the frame with the page and shoot straight on.

What page size does it use?

Letter in the US and Canada, A4 elsewhere, based on your browser's language. You can change it, or choose Fit to image.

Can I scan several pages into one PDF?

Yes. Take as many photos as you need; each becomes a page in one PDF, in the order shown.
