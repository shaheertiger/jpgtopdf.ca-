## How to convert JPG to PDF

1. **Select your images.** Tap the box or drop files onto it. You can mix JPG, PNG and HEIC, and add more later.
2. **Put the pages in order.** Drag thumbnails, or use the ← and → buttons. The order on screen is the page order in the PDF; this is the step people skip, then wonder why page 3 comes first.
3. **Fix any sideways photo.** Tap ↻ to turn a page 90°. Photos that your phone tagged as rotated are already shown the right way up.
4. **Choose page size and quality.** Keep **Fit to image** and **Original** for the sharpest file, or pick A4 or Letter for printing and **Balanced** for email.
5. **Download.** Press **Download PDF**. The file is built on your device and saved straight to your downloads.

## Does converting JPG to PDF lose quality?

Not on the **Original** setting. A JPEG is already compressed, and a PDF can hold that exact JPEG data. This converter copies the bytes in unchanged instead of decoding and re-saving the picture, so the image in the PDF is identical to your file. PNG images are stored losslessly too.

Phone photos often carry a rotation tag (EXIF) instead of being stored upright. Many converters re-save the photo to rotate it, which costs a little quality each time. Here the common rotations (90°, 180°, 270°) are applied with a page transform, so the JPEG still isn't re-encoded. Only mirrored photos, which are rare, are redrawn at high quality.

Quality is lost only when you ask for it, with the **Balanced** or **Small** presets. For more on keeping images sharp, read [JPG to PDF without losing quality](/blog/jpg-to-pdf-without-losing-quality/).

## Which quality setting should I choose?

A 2,480-pixel long edge is still over 200 dpi on a full Letter or A4 page, so text in a photographed document stays readable on Balanced. Small is still fine on screen but can look soft when printed.

## Why is my PDF so big, and how do I make it smaller?

A modern phone photo is 2–5 MB, so ten photos make a 20–50 MB PDF on the Original setting. That's the size of the pictures, not waste added by the PDF. Pick **Balanced** before you press Download and the same photos usually land at a fraction of that size. If you already made the PDF, run it through [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf/). The [JPG to PDF too big](/blog/jpg-to-pdf-file-size/) guide explains the numbers.

## Use cases

### Gmail and Outlook attachments

Gmail attachments are capped at 25 MB and Outlook at 20 MB. Ten phone photos on Original can exceed both; Balanced usually brings them well under.

### IRCC and other government portals

IRCC uploads have a per-file limit of about 4 MB on many forms. Use A4 or Letter pages, the Balanced or Small preset, and check the size shown before uploading. See [IRCC documents as PDF](/blog/ircc-documents-pdf/).

### Job applications

Upload portals often want one PDF for a certificate or ID. Photograph each page, convert them in order, and keep the file small with Balanced.

### Receipts and expense reports

Snap each receipt, select them all, and send one PDF to accounting instead of a dozen photos.

### HEIC photos from an iPhone

HEIC files are decoded in the browser and placed in the PDF like any other image, so you don't have to convert them to JPG first. For a page just about iPhone photos, see [HEIC to PDF](/heic-to-pdf/).
