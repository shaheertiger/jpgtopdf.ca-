## How to compress a PDF

1. **Add the PDF.** Tap **Select a PDF** or drop the file on the box. It opens on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.
2. **Pick a level — not the strongest by default.** Recommended suits most documents. Use Strong only when Recommended is still too big: it softens photos.
3. **Or choose Target size.** Type the limit you have to meet (for example 2 MB) or pick one from the list. The tool tries Light, then Recommended, then Strong, and stops at the first one that fits.
4. **Tick greyscale for text scans.** Black-and-white documents lose nothing visible and get smaller still.
5. **Press Compress PDF.** The new size and the percentage saved appear, and the file downloads. If the browser blocks the download, use the **Download** button.

## How much smaller will my PDF get?

It depends on what is inside. We measured each level on ten real PDFs: phone-photo PDFs, a 300 DPI scan, a research paper, tax forms and a brochure. Typical results:

| PDF type | Light | Recommended | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone photos saved as PDF (9 MB, 4 pages) | 56% smaller | 85% smaller | 94% smaller |
| Scanned document, 300 DPI (13.9 MB, 8 pages) | 45% smaller | 74% smaller | 87% smaller |
| Photo PDF that was already compressed once (2.9 MB) | no change | 54% smaller | 82% smaller |
| Typed or exported document (Word, tax form, paper) | 0–8% | 0–8% | 0–8% |

**What each level does.** Light keeps pictures up to 3,000 pixels on the long side at high JPEG quality: best for photos you will print. Recommended keeps 2,000 pixels at good quality, still about 180 DPI for a full scanned page, so text stays crisp on screen and on an office printer. Strong keeps 1,400 pixels at lower quality: best for upload portals that only need the document to be legible.

### Why does a text PDF barely shrink?

Text in a PDF is stored as letters and font outlines, which already take very little room. A 20-page report exported from Word is often under 500 KB. If such a file is still too large, the size comes from something else: embedded fonts, an attached file, or a few big images. When there is nothing to compress, the tool tells you so instead of handing back the same file.

### Scanned, searchable and digital PDFs

- **Scanned PDF** (every page is a picture): compresses the most, often 70–90%. Tick greyscale for black-and-white pages.
- **Searchable scan** (a picture with a hidden text layer, from OCR): compresses like a scan, and the hidden text layer is kept, so it stays searchable.
- **Digital PDF** (exported from Word, Google Docs or a website): only its photos shrink; the text is already compact.

## How do I compress a PDF to a specific size?

Choose **Target size**, type the number and pick MB or KB. The tool runs the levels from lightest to strongest and keeps the first result under your limit, so you get the best quality that fits. If even the strongest setting is too big, it switches images to greyscale as a last step and says so. Here is what is realistic:

### Compress a PDF to under 2 MB

Most phone-photo and scanned PDFs up to about 15 pages get under 2 MB with Recommended or Strong. A 300 DPI scan of 8 pages went from 13.9 MB to 1.8 MB with Strong in our test.

### Compress a PDF to under 1 MB

Usually reachable for up to 6–10 scanned pages. The target-size mode took our 8-page scan to 859 KB, still readable. Longer scans may need splitting.

### Compress a PDF to 500 KB

Realistic for 1–4 scanned pages or a few photos. Tick greyscale; colour photos take more room than black-and-white text.

### Compress a PDF to 200 KB

Possible for one or two scanned pages of text in greyscale. A long document will not fit in 200 KB without becoming unreadable: send fewer pages, or [split the PDF by size](/split-pdf-by-size/).

## Which limit do I need to meet?

These are the limits people most often compress for. Check the number shown on the upload page, because services change them.

### Gmail (25 MB)

Gmail attachments can total 25 MB per email; anything larger is turned into a Google Drive link. Recommended almost always gets a scanned PDF well under it, so the file arrives as a real attachment.

### Outlook (20 MB)

Outlook.com and many work accounts stop at 20 MB per message, and some company servers set 10 MB. Aim for half the limit if you are attaching more than one file.

### Job application portals (often 2–5 MB)

Applicant tracking systems usually accept a CV and cover letter of 2 to 5 MB per file. A CV exported from Word is already small; a scanned diploma or reference letter is what usually needs compressing. Recommended, plus greyscale for text pages, is the safe choice.

### IRCC and visa uploads (4 MB per file)

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada accounts accept PDFs of about 4 MB per file (some forms allow 5 MB). Our [IRCC guide](/blog/ircc-documents-pdf/) covers the details. US visa and NVC document uploads use small per-file limits too; check the number on the upload page and use Target size.

### WhatsApp and text messages

WhatsApp accepts documents up to 2 GB, so size is rarely blocked, but a 2 MB PDF opens quickly on the other person's phone and costs less mobile data than a 30 MB one. Recommended is enough.

### Court and government e-filing

E-filing portals often cap each file at 5–10 MB and reject image-only PDFs. Compress first, then run [OCR PDF](/ocr-pdf/) if the portal asks for searchable text.

## What happens to quality?

Only the pictures inside the PDF change. Text, fonts, lines, tables and charts drawn as vectors are copied unchanged, so typed text stays perfectly sharp and you can still select and search it. Photos and scanned pages are resized to the level's pixel limit and saved as JPEG.

You will not see a difference on screen with Light or Recommended. With Strong, zoom in on photos and you may notice softness; scanned text stays readable. The tool never makes a file bigger: if a picture would not shrink by at least 8%, the original picture is kept.

### Why is compression done in the browser?

Most compression sites upload your PDF to their server, which matters for CVs, IDs, tax returns and contracts. Here, the PDF is processed by JavaScript on your own device. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it still works. [How it works](/how-it-works/) explains the details.
