## Quick answer

Drop your images, keep **Recommended** (JPEG quality 72) or choose **Target size** and type a limit such as 200 KB, then press **Compress images**. Setting a maximum width such as 1920 px saves the most, because a phone photo is often 4,000 pixels wide. Re-saving also strips EXIF data, including GPS location. If re-saving at full size would make a file bigger, the original is kept.

## How to compress an image

1. **Add your images.** One or many; HEIC photos from an iPhone work too.
2. **Choose a quality, or a target size.** Recommended suits most uses. Pick **Target size…** and type the limit in KB when a form gives you one.
3. **Set a maximum width if the image is large.** This is the step people miss: quality alone can't take a 12-megapixel photo to 100 KB, but 1080 px wide at quality 72 usually can.
4. **Pick the format.** Auto saves JPG, or WebP when the image has transparency (so the see-through background survives).
5. **Compress and download.** The list shows each file's old and new size. Several files download as a ZIP.

## How do I compress an image to under 200 KB, 100 KB or 50 KB?

Choose **Target size…** and type the number of KB. The tool tries up to eight quality settings between 10 and 95 and keeps the highest one that fits. If even the lowest quality is too big, it tells you, and the fix is a smaller maximum width.

| Target | Suggested max width | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 MB | Keep size or 3840 px | Full-size photo, clean |
| 500 KB | 2048 px | Sharp on any screen |
| 200 KB | 1440 or 1080 px | Good for forms and the web |
| 100 KB | 1080 px | Fine for profile and ID uploads |
| 50 KB | 800 px | Small thumbnails, strict portals |

These widths are starting points; detailed photos need a smaller width than plain ones for the same size.

## Why is a PNG not getting smaller?

PNG is lossless, so re-saving it as PNG rarely shrinks it. Choose **Auto** or **JPG**: a PNG photo or screenshot saved as JPG is usually far smaller. If the image has a transparent background, Auto picks WebP, which keeps the transparency and still compresses.

## Use cases

### Job portals and application forms

Many ask for a photo or scan under a set size, often 200 KB or 1 MB. Use Target size with a width of 1080–1440 px.

### US visa photo (DS-160)

The State Department asks for a JPEG of at most 240 KB, square, at least 600×600 pixels. Crop it square with [Resize image](/resize-image/) first, then compress to about 200 KB.

### Discord

Free Discord accounts have a small upload limit (10 MB at the time of writing). Photos over it upload fine after compressing to Recommended at 2048 px.

### Email and WhatsApp

Gmail attachments are limited to 25 MB in total. Compressing a batch to 1920 px lets you send many more photos in one message.

### Your own website

Big images slow pages down. 1920 px at quality 72, or WebP, is a good default for full-width photos.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Could not reach the target" | Pick a smaller max width | Quality alone has a floor; fewer pixels is the bigger lever |
| File got bigger | Choose JPG instead of PNG, or keep the original | PNG output is lossless |
| Transparent background turned white | Choose Auto or WebP | JPG has no transparency |
| Output is PNG though WebP was chosen | Use a current Chrome, Edge or Firefox | That browser can't save WebP |
| Colours look slightly different | Use High quality | Strong compression shifts subtle tones |
