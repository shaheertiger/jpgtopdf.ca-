You're uploading passports, transcripts or bank statements to your IRCC account, and the file is rejected: too big, or the wrong format, or you have five pages and one upload slot.

**Make one PDF per document, under your account's limit.** IRCC lists 4 MB per file for the IRCC secure account, citizenship and Permanent Residence Portal, and 5 MB for the IRCC Portal. Scan or photograph each page, resize to roughly 1,100–1,600 pixels on the long side, then combine them into one PDF with [our free converter](/). Nothing is uploaded until you upload it to IRCC yourself.

The problem is that a phone photo of one page is 2–5 MB on its own. This guide covers the official limits, IRCC's own resolution advice, and a workflow that keeps passports and statements readable and under the limit.

## What Are IRCC's File Size Limits?

From IRCC's Help Centre (page modified November 3, 2026):

| Online account type | Limit per file |
|---|---|
| IRCC secure account | 4 MB |
| IRCC Portal | 5 MB |
| IRCC Portal – new version | 5 MB |
| Online citizenship application | 4 MB |
| Asylum portal | 4 MB |
| Permanent Residence Portal | 4 MB |

Source: [IRCC Help Centre, file size limits](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=1123). Limits change, and IRCC notes some can be as low as 2 MB, so always use the limit shown in your own account.

### What Most People Don't Know

IRCC's own advice is to use **low resolution**: about 96 dpi for scanned JPG and PNG files, and 96 or 150 dpi for images inside PDFs ([IRCC: reducing file size](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=1213)). A phone photo is far above that, which is why files are rejected.

> **Key point:** Readable is what counts, not maximum detail. A 150 dpi Letter page is about 1,275 × 1,650 pixels, which is plenty for an officer to read.

## How to Turn Photos Into an IRCC-Ready PDF

### Step 1: Check Your Limit and Slot

Open the upload slot in your account and note the size limit and what it asks for (for example, "Passport: all pages with stamps"). One slot usually means one file, so multi-page documents go in one PDF.

### Step 2: Scan or Photograph Each Page Flat

Use your phone's scanner for paper: iPhone Files → ⋯ → Scan Documents, or Google Drive → Scan on Android ([full guide](/blog/scan-document-to-pdf-phone/)). Scans are cropped and much smaller than photos. Keep **Colour** for passports, IDs and stamped documents.

### Step 3: Resize to About 1,100–1,600 Pixels, Not More

If you used the camera, resize each photo so the long side is roughly 1,100–1,600 px. That's between IRCC's 96 and 150 dpi guidance for a Letter page. How to resize on each device: [JPG to PDF too big?](/blog/jpg-to-pdf-file-size/).

> **Pro tip:** Name files before combining: `01-passport-bio.jpg`, `02-passport-stamps.jpg`. It keeps the order right and makes it obvious if a page is missing.

### Step 4: Combine Into One PDF

Open [jpgtopdf.ca](/), select all pages for that document, drag them into order, choose **Letter**, and tap **Download PDF**. Your documents are processed in your browser, not sent to a server.

### Step 5: Check Size and Readability

Confirm the file is under the limit, then zoom to 100% and read the smallest text: passport numbers, dates, stamps. If it's unreadable, use a bit more resolution and split the document only if IRCC's instructions allow it.

## Common IRCC Upload Problems

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| "File exceeds maximum size" | Resize pages or scan in greyscale, then recombine | Size is the sum of the images |
| Several documents, one slot | Combine them into one PDF in a logical order | One slot accepts one file |
| Officer can't read stamps | Rescan in colour with even light | Faint stamps vanish in B&W |
| Photo is a HEIC file | Pick it through the browser or set iPhone Camera to Most Compatible | Converts it to JPG |
| PDF won't open after compressing | Recreate it from the images | Some compressors damage PDFs |

## Should I Upload a JPG or a PDF?

IRCC's portals accept several formats, but which ones vary by account, so check yours. A PDF is usually the safer choice for anything with more than one page: one file, fixed order, and it opens the same for every officer.

## Compression Websites (Use With Caution)

Many applicants search "compress PDF under 4 MB" and upload their passport to the first site they find.

### The Real Risks

- A copy of your passport, bank statements and police certificates on someone else's server.
- Over-compression that makes numbers unreadable, which can delay a file.

Safety checklist:

- Prefer tools that work in your browser without uploading.
- Resize images yourself before using any compressor.
- Open the final PDF and read every number before you submit.

> **Bottom line:** For immigration documents, keep the files on your own device until you upload them to IRCC.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the IRCC file size limit?

It depends on the account: 4 MB per file for the IRCC secure account, citizenship application, asylum portal and PR Portal, and 5 MB for the IRCC Portal, according to IRCC's Help Centre. Check your own account.

### What resolution should IRCC documents be?

IRCC suggests about 96 dpi for scanned images and 96 or 150 dpi for images in PDFs. Make sure the text is still readable.

### How do I combine multiple pages into one PDF for IRCC?

Select all the page images in a converter, order them, and download one PDF. See [combine JPGs into one PDF](/blog/combine-jpg-into-one-pdf/).

### Can I take photos of my documents with my phone?

Yes, but use the phone's document scanner if possible, and crop out the background. Make sure every edge and stamp is visible.

### Is it safe to use online tools for IRCC documents?

Prefer tools that process files on your device. Upload-based tools keep a copy of sensitive documents on their servers.

### My PDF is still over 4 MB. What now?

Resize the pages further, scan in greyscale where colour isn't needed, and remove blank pages. Compress only as a last step.
