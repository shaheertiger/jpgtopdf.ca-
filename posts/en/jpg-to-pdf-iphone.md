You need to send a picture from your iPhone as a PDF: a signed form, a receipt, a page of notes. Photos has no "Save as PDF" button, so it looks impossible.

**The quickest built-in way is the Files app:** save the photo to Files, long-press it and tap **Create PDF**. It takes about 30 seconds and works for several photos at once. If you'd rather stay in Safari, open [our free converter](/), select the photos and tap Download PDF.

Apple hides PDF creation inside Files and the Print screen, so most people never find it. This guide walks through three free methods, explains what to do about HEIC photos, and shows which one keeps the picture sharpest.

## Why Doesn't the Photos App Save as PDF?

Photos is built for images, and PDF is a document format. Apple put PDF tools where documents live: the Files app and the system Print screen. Both are on every iPhone.

### What Most People Don't Know

Since iOS 11, the iPhone camera saves photos as **HEIC** ("High Efficiency") by default, not JPG. Some websites and older software reject HEIC. You don't usually need to change anything: when you pick a photo through Safari's file picker, iOS normally hands the site a JPEG copy, and the Files app's Create PDF accepts HEIC directly.

> **Key point:** Want every new photo saved as JPG? Go to Settings → Camera → Formats → **Most Compatible**. Existing photos stay as they are.

## Method 1: Files App "Create PDF" (Built In)

### Step 1: Save the Picture to Files

Open Photos, select the picture (or tap **Select** and choose several), tap **Share**, then **Save to Files**. Pick a folder such as On My iPhone → Downloads.

### Step 2: Find It in the Files App

Open **Files** and go to the folder you chose.

### Step 3: Long-Press, Then Create PDF

Long-press the picture and choose **Create PDF** (it may sit under **Quick Actions**). For several photos, tap **⋯** (top right) → **Select**, choose them, tap **⋯** at the bottom → **Create PDF**. They're combined into one PDF in name order.

> **Pro tip:** Files sorts by name, so photos named IMG_4521, IMG_4522… come out in shooting order. Rename them 1, 2, 3 first if you need a different order.

### Step 4: Rename and Share

The PDF appears next to the photo. Long-press → **Rename**, then **Share** to Mail, Messages or a website upload.

## Method 2: The Print Trick

This turns anything you can print into a PDF.

1. In Photos, select the picture(s) and tap **Share** → **Print**.
2. On Print Options, tap the **Share** button at the top. On older iOS versions, pinch outward on the page preview first to open it full screen, then tap Share.
3. Choose **Save to Files** (or send it straight to Mail).

The result is a Letter-size page with your photo fitted to it, which is handy for printing. Menu labels change between iOS versions, so look for the nearest match.

## Method 3: Safari and a Browser Converter

1. Open [jpgtopdf.ca](/) in Safari.
2. Tap **Select images** → **Photo Library**, and choose your pictures in the order you want them.
3. Pick **Letter** if it will be printed, or keep **Fit to image**.
4. Tap **Download PDF**, then open the download and tap Share → **Save to Files**.

The photos are converted on your iPhone, not uploaded. This is the only one of the three that lets you set margins and orientation.

## Troubleshooting on iPhone

| Problem | Fix | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| No "Create PDF" option | Update iOS, or use the Print trick | Create PDF arrived with iOS 15 Quick Actions |
| PDF saved but you can't find it | Files → Recents, or Browse → Downloads | Safari downloads go to the Downloads folder |
| Website says the file type is wrong | Pick the photo through Safari's file picker, or set Camera to Most Compatible | Converts HEIC to JPEG |
| PDF is huge | Use Method 3 with fewer photos, or see [why the PDF is so big](/blog/jpg-to-pdf-file-size/) | Each iPhone photo is 2–5 MB |
| Document looks skewed | Use Notes or Files → Scan Documents instead | The scanner flattens and crops the page |

## For Paper Documents, Scan Instead

If the "picture" is a sheet of paper, don't photograph it: scan it. In **Files**, tap **⋯** → **Scan Documents**, or in **Notes**, tap the attachment icon → **Scan Documents**. The iPhone finds the edges, flattens the page and saves a PDF directly. Full guide: [how to scan a document to PDF with your phone](/blog/scan-document-to-pdf-phone/).

## Upload-Based Converter Apps (Use With Caution)

The App Store is full of "PDF converter" apps. Many upload your photos to their servers, show ads, or ask for a weekly subscription after a few conversions.

### The Real Risks

- A copy of your ID or bank statement on a server you can't check.
- Subscriptions that renew automatically, often at a high weekly price.
- Full photo library access when the app only needed one picture.

Before installing, check: does it work offline? Does it ask for "Full Access" to Photos? Is the price weekly?

> **Bottom line:** The iPhone already does this for free. Use Files, Print or a browser tool that keeps photos on the phone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I convert a JPG to PDF on iPhone for free?

Save the JPG to the Files app, long-press it and tap Create PDF. It's built in and free. You can also use a browser converter in Safari.

### Can I combine several pictures into one PDF on iPhone?

Yes. In Files, select the pictures, tap ⋯ and choose Create PDF; they're merged in name order. In a browser converter you choose the order yourself.

### Where does the PDF go on my iPhone?

Create PDF saves it next to the original in Files. Safari downloads go to Files → Downloads.

### Do I need to convert HEIC to JPG first?

Usually not. Files' Create PDF accepts HEIC, and Safari's file picker normally gives websites a JPEG copy. Set Camera → Formats → Most Compatible to shoot JPG from now on.

### Does this work on iPad?

Yes. The Files app, the Print trick and Safari work the same way on iPad.

### Will the PDF lose quality?

Create PDF and our converter keep the original image. The Print trick fits the photo onto a Letter page, which can resize it slightly.
