## Where Does JPG Quality Get Lost?

JPG is lossy: every time a JPG is re-saved, it loses a little more detail. Converting to PDF only hurts if the tool re-saves the image.

### What Most People Don't Know

The biggest losses usually happen **before** conversion. WhatsApp, Messenger and many email apps shrink photos when you send them. If you convert the copy someone sent you, you're converting a reduced version. Go back to the original on the phone that took it.

> **Key point:** You can't add detail back. The PDF can only be as sharp as the JPG you give it.

## How to Convert JPG to PDF at Full Quality

### Step 1: Use the Original File

Use the photo from the camera roll or the original download, not a copy from a messaging app or a screenshot of the photo.

### Step 2: Choose a Converter That Doesn't Re-Encode

[jpgtopdf.ca](/) embeds the JPG data directly in the PDF. Print-based routes (Microsoft Print to PDF, iPhone's Print trick) redraw the image for a page, which can soften fine text.

### Step 3: Pick "Fit to Image"

**Fit to image** makes the page match the photo's pixels, so nothing is scaled down. Letter or A4 shrink the image to fit the page visually, but our converter still keeps every pixel: you can zoom in and see the full detail.

> **Pro tip:** Never press "compress" or pick "small file size" if quality is the goal. Compression is how most sites shrink PDFs: they re-save every image at lower quality.

### Step 4: Zoom to 200% and Check

Open the PDF, zoom to 200% on the smallest text, and compare it with the photo at the same zoom. If both look the same, nothing was lost.

## Quality vs File Size: The Trade-Off

Full quality means a bigger file. A 12-megapixel phone photo is typically 2–5 MB, and the PDF will be about the same. If an upload limit forces a smaller file, shrink in a controlled way:

| Goal | Do this | Quality effect |
|---|---|---|
| Keep text readable, halve size | Resize photo to ~2000 px on the long side | Barely visible on screen |
| Stay under 2 MB for a few pages | Resize to ~1600 px, then convert | Fine for documents |
| Smallest possible | Compress the finished PDF | Visible softening |

## "HD" or "Enhance" Converters (Use With Caution)

Some tools promise "HD PDF" or "AI enhance". Enhancement invents detail that wasn't in the photo, which can change numbers or letters, a real problem for IDs, receipts and contracts.

### The Real Risks

- Altered digits or characters on documents.
- Uploading private documents to process them.

> **Bottom line:** For documents, faithful beats "enhanced". Keep the original pixels.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does converting JPG to PDF reduce quality?

Not if the converter embeds the JPG as-is, as jpgtopdf.ca does. Print to PDF and compression options can reduce it.

### What resolution should a JPG be for a PDF?

For documents viewed on screen, 1600–2500 px on the long side is plenty. For printing a Letter page, about 2550 × 3300 px gives 300 dpi.

### Why is my PDF blurry when the photo is sharp?

The tool re-compressed the image, or you converted a reduced copy sent through a chat app.

### Is PNG better than JPG for PDF quality?

For screenshots and text, yes: PNG is lossless. For photos, a good JPG looks the same and is much smaller.

### How do I check if quality was lost?

Zoom both the photo and the PDF to 200% on the smallest text and compare.
