What to look for in a JPG to PDF converter

1. It works in your browser (no upload)

This is the most important feature. The safest converters do everything locally in your browser, so your images never leave your device. That matters a great deal in Canada when you are converting tax slips, ID, or medical paperwork. If a tool uploads your files to “process” them, your private documents sit on someone else’s server.

2. Truly free — no watermark, no limit

Plenty of “free” tools stamp a watermark on your PDF or cap you at a few files before asking for payment. A genuinely free converter has no sign-up, no watermark and no file limit.

3. It combines multiple images

Converting one image is easy. The real time-saver is merging several JPGs into one PDF and reordering the pages. Look for drag-to-reorder and the ability to remove pages before you download.

4. It keeps quality high

A good converter embeds images at their original resolution rather than recompressing them, so text stays sharp and photos stay crisp.

5. It works on mobile

Most conversions start on a phone — a photo of a receipt, a screenshot, a snap of a form. The tool should work in Safari and Chrome on iPhone and Android with no app install.
