HEIC to PDF

Quick answer

Drop your iPhone HEIC photos into the box, drag them into order and press Download PDF. Each photo is decoded in your browser, saved as a high-quality JPEG (quality 92) and placed on its own page. You can mix in JPG and PNG files. Nothing is uploaded, and the Balanced preset is typically 60–80% smaller for upload forms.

How to convert HEIC to PDF

  1. Select your HEIC photos. Pick them from Files, Photos or a folder of AirDropped pictures. JPG and PNG files can go in the same batch.
  2. Wait for the thumbnails. Each HEIC file is decoded first; on a computer without a built-in HEIC decoder this takes a second or two per photo.
  3. Set the order. Drag the thumbnails or use ← and →. Tap ↻ to turn a page.
  4. Pick a page size and quality. Fit to image keeps each page the shape of the photo. Choose Balanced if the PDF has to go through email or an upload form.
  5. Download the PDF. Press Download PDF and the file saves to your device.

Why won't my HEIC photos open on Windows or in forms?

HEIC is the format iPhones use by default since iOS 11. It stores a photo in about half the space of a JPEG, but many Windows PCs, Android phones and upload portals can't open it. A PDF opens everywhere, which is why forms ask for one.

Safari can read HEIC itself, so on a Mac or iPhone the photo is decoded by the browser. Chrome, Edge and Firefox can't, so the page loads a HEIC decoder (libheif, about 1.4 MB, from this site) the first time you add a HEIC file.

Use cases

Rental and job applications

Landlords and HR portals usually want one PDF of your ID, pay stubs or certificates. Photograph them, convert, done.

IRCC and visa uploads

Many IRCC upload slots accept PDF files of about 4 MB. Use Letter or A4 pages with the Balanced or Small preset.

Emailing photos from an iPhone to a Windows user

A PDF avoids the "I can't open this file" reply. Gmail attachments top out at 25 MB, so use Balanced for more than a few photos.

Frequently asked questions

Is HEIC to PDF free?

Yes. No account, no watermark and no limit on how often you use it.

Are my iPhone photos uploaded?

No. Decoding and building the PDF both happen in your browser, so your photos stay on your device.

Do I need to convert HEIC to JPG first?

No. Add the HEIC files directly; they are converted to JPEG inside the browser while the PDF is made.

Does it work on Windows?

Yes. In Chrome, Edge or Firefox on Windows, the page loads its own HEIC decoder, so you don't need Microsoft's HEIF extension.

Is there any quality loss?

A little, by design: the HEIC photo becomes a JPEG at quality 92, which looks the same to the eye. Choosing Balanced or Small reduces size further.

Can I mix HEIC and JPG in one PDF?

Yes. Any mix of HEIC, JPG, PNG and WebP works, in the order you set.
