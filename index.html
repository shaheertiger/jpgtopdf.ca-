How to convert JPG to PDF

  1. Select your images. Tap the box or drop files onto it. You can mix JPG, PNG and HEIC, and add more later.
  2. Put the pages in order. Drag thumbnails, or use the ← and → buttons. The order on screen is the page order in the PDF; this is the step people skip, then wonder why page 3 comes first.
  3. Fix any sideways photo. Tap ↻ to turn a page 90°. Photos that your phone tagged as rotated are already shown the right way up.
  4. Choose page size and quality. Keep Fit to image and Original for the sharpest file, or pick A4 or Letter for printing and Balanced for email.
  5. Download. Press Download PDF. The file is built on your device and saved straight to your downloads.

Does converting JPG to PDF lose quality?

Not on the Original setting. A JPEG is already compressed, and a PDF can hold that exact JPEG data. This converter copies the bytes in unchanged instead of decoding and re-saving the picture, so the image in the PDF is identical to your file. PNG images are stored losslessly too.

Phone photos often carry a rotation tag (EXIF) instead of being stored upright. Many converters re-save the photo to rotate it, which costs a little quality each time. Here the common rotations (90°, 180°, 270°) are applied with a page transform, so the JPEG still isn't re-encoded. Only mirrored photos, which are rare, are redrawn at high quality.

Quality is lost only when you ask for it, with the Balanced or Small presets. For more on keeping images sharp, read JPG to PDF without losing quality.

How do I combine several JPGs into one PDF?

Select all the images at once (Ctrl or Cmd-click on a computer, or pick several in your phone's photo picker), or add them in batches: new images are added to the end of the list. Every image becomes its own page in a single PDF. The guide combine JPGs into one PDF covers the same job with built-in apps on each device.

If you already have PDFs and want to join them, use Merge PDF instead.

Why is my PDF so big, and how do I make it smaller?

A modern phone photo is 2–5 MB, so ten photos make a 20–50 MB PDF on the Original setting. That's the size of the pictures, not waste added by the PDF. Pick Balanced before you press Download and the same photos usually land at a fraction of that size. If you already made the PDF, run it through Compress PDF. The JPG to PDF too big guide explains the numbers.

Use cases

Gmail and Outlook attachments

Gmail attachments are capped at 25 MB and Outlook at 20 MB. Ten phone photos on Original can exceed both; Balanced usually brings them well under.

IRCC and other government portals

IRCC uploads have a per-file limit of about 4 MB on many forms. Use A4 or Letter pages, the Balanced or Small preset, and check the size shown before uploading. See IRCC documents as PDF.

Job applications

Upload portals often want one PDF for a certificate or ID. Photograph each page, convert them in order, and keep the file small with Balanced.

Receipts and expense reports

Snap each receipt, select them all, and send one PDF to accounting instead of a dozen photos.

HEIC photos from an iPhone

HEIC files are decoded in the browser and placed in the PDF like any other image, so you don't have to convert them to JPG first. For a page just about iPhone photos, see HEIC to PDF.

Frequently asked questions

Is this JPG to PDF converter free?

Yes. It is free with no account, no watermark and no daily limit; the site is kept free by its owners at pdfcanada.ca.

Are my images uploaded to a server?

No. The PDF is built by JavaScript in your browser, so your images never leave your device, and the page keeps working offline once it has loaded.

Can I convert PNG, WebP or HEIC as well as JPG?

Yes. JPG, JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP and HEIC can be mixed in one PDF. PNG stays lossless; WebP is saved as high-quality JPEG; HEIC is decoded in the browser.

Will the PDF be the same quality as my photos?

Yes, on the Original setting. JPEG data is copied into the PDF unchanged and phone rotation is applied without re-saving, so nothing is recompressed.

How many images can I put in one PDF?

There is no set number; your device's memory is the limit. A laptop handles dozens of phone photos easily; on an older phone, convert in smaller batches if the page slows down.

What page size should I use?

Use Fit to image for sharing on screen, since each page matches the photo. Use A4 (most of the world) or Letter (US and Canada) when the PDF will be printed or a form asks for a standard page.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. It runs in Safari, Chrome and other current mobile browsers, and you can pick images straight from your photo library. Phone guides: iPhone and Android.

Can I convert a PDF back to JPG?

Yes, with PDF to JPG, which saves each page as an image.
